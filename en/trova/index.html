The Nueva Trova

Twenty years documenting a song movement, from inside the room.

What this is

The Nueva Trova is the Cuban song movement that emerged publicly in February 1967 and went on to define the sound and the argument of several generations: Silvio Rodríguez, Pablo Milanés, Noel Nicola, Vicente and Santiago Feliú, and the three waves of songwriters who followed them. It is studied in Latin American departments, in ethnomusicology and in cultural history.

Between 2001 and 2019 Antonio López Sánchez published three books on it. He is not a musician. He is a journalist with a degree in Social Communication from the University of Havana who spent two decades interviewing the people who made this music, writing about their records in the Cuban cultural press, and, on occasion, reading his own poems on stage between their guitars.

The three books do not repeat each other. Each one asks a question the previous one left open.

The books

2001

La canción de la Nueva Trova (The Song of the Nueva Trova)

Atril Ediciones Musicales · Essays and interviews

The author's first book, and a history of the movement told with the voices of its own protagonists, from its public appearance in February 1967 onwards. It reads the three successive waves of songwriters, what separates them and what they share, and it argues that the guitar remained the centre of gravity even when other instruments arrived. Written close to the source, with interviews given by the founders themselves.

See the book

2008

Trovadoras (Women of the Trova)

Editorial Oriente · Interviews

Working women songwriters speak about the place they, and the women before them, have held in Cuban song. Alongside them the book carries two specialists: Isabel Moya on gender and the musicographer Lino Betancourt. For a gender studies or Latin American music list this is a primary source in interview form, and there is very little like it in any language.

See the book

2019

Convertida en canción (Turned into Song: the image of women in the lyrics of the Nueva Trova)

Editorial Capiro · Essay

Which woman are our troubadours singing to? Have the women in those sung portraits changed, or grown? The book reads the lyrics as a running argument with Cuban society and follows what they say about women entering public life, about sexuality inside and outside couples, about sex work, about breakups and infidelity, and about homosexual love. It is the most directly citable of the three for a university press, and the one that travels furthest from Cuba.

Close to the song

The research was not done from a distance. The author was also invited into the movement's own spaces, and the site keeps the recordings that survive.

  • La impúdica es la guitarra, a concert in the A Guitarra Limpia cycle at the Centro Cultural Pablo de la Torriente Brau, Havana, 2000, where he read his poems between the songs.
  • Two radio programmes with Fernando Rodríguez Sosa on Habana Radio, 2018. The station is now entirely offline, and these survive in this archive and in the Internet Archive.
  • The television programme Entre libros, 2019.
  • Work in the Cuban music press: AM PM Magazine, La Jiribilla, El Caimán Barbudo, La Gaceta de Cuba, and the cultural sections of the magazines Mujeres and Muchacha from 2001 to 2010.

For publishers and editors

World rights outside Cuba are available for all three titles, separately or as a set. The three together make a twenty-year account of one movement, written from inside it, which is a stronger proposition than any of them alone.

These are the shortest books to bring into English on this list: essays and interviews rather than novels, and a subject with a defined academic readership. Full manuscripts and synopses are available on request.
