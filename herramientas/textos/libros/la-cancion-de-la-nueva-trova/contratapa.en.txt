With the voices of several of its principal protagonists, here is a view of the history of the Nueva Trova, from its first public appearance in February 1967 to the present day. When the troubadours emerged in the national culture, they did so as an expression of both rupture and continuity. They raised, and still raise, the song and the music of their time.
Three generations of troubadours can be seen across those years, with their differences and their likenesses. Love, history, daily life, dreams, social and personal conflict are all present in their songs. And all of them, even those who sought the shelter of other instruments, show that the guitar does not grow old.
What defines the Nueva Trova, says Silvio Rodríguez, is "the shared identity of an era, and that generation's need to accept itself and to speak." Noel Nicola, for his part, calls it "the most original cultural movement, the most our own" in the development of Cuban culture. "Troubadours are still the ones who plant a guitar in front of their heart," says Augusto Blanca, to leave a record of that continuity.
These and other questions are taken up in the book you hold in your hands, which does not claim to exhaust every side of this story, but does set out to lay bare its essentials.
