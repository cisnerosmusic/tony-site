In essence, this book is the thesis with which I earned my degree in Social Communication, in 1998. It was published, of course, without the theoretical appendices an academic exercise requires, and with the adjustments needed to bring it to a general reader. It contains a long historical essay on the origins of the Nueva Trova as a creative current, and a series of interviews with several of its founders and with figures who took a direct part in those beginnings or made decisive contributions to them.
Thanks to the boundless goodwill of the troubadour Noel Nicola, and of the great journalist Manolito González Bello, who served as editor, and to whom I remain grateful for the gesture, the book came out in 2001.
It is, however, a piece of research that has never stopped growing, both with new interviews over the years and with new historical evidence and analysis. I have always thought, too, that some interviews were left pending, by the grace of bureaucracies, of silences in place of answers, of departures, and even of deaths.
Now, decades later, it may be time to go over its contents once and for all and try to publish a new, updated edition.
