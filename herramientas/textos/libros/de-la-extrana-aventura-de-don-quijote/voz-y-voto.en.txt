After many years, and books in Cuba can take that long and longer to be published, my wish to have a Cuban edition of this text was at last fulfilled. As I have said elsewhere, this story won First Prize in the Reescribir El Quijote en Cuba competition in 2005, held by the Embassy of Spain in Cuba and the Spanish Agency for International Cooperation to mark the four hundredth anniversary of the publication of Miguel de Cervantes' El ingenioso hidalgo Don Quijote de la Mancha.
Although an anthology of the prizes and mentions was published in Spain, every writer wishes, perhaps not to be a prophet, but at least to be read in his own land. So, after much rowing, nearly two decades of acceptances, oversights and silent postponements, the book was published in digital form at home. It turned out to be the first of this author's titles to appear in that new form.
Despite the poor connections and other trials of the Cuban internet, readers did at last reach the book. Others, thanks to copies the writer sent directly, have joined in the reading, with the occasional word of praise. Once again, and fortunately, opinions on this literary divertimento have been kind, for which I am grateful. Another triumph was the lovely gesture of the cover illustration, a gift from my inimitable Iván Batista. Thank you for that birth, and for that Quixote and Sancho made of pure real life and, at the same time, fantastic.
Among the guardian angels of my literary life, some tangible and real, others loving but distant, and others simply imagined or dreamed, is without doubt the Knight of the Sorrowful Countenance. I thank Don Quixote, and his earthly Sancho Panza, for agreeing to travel my pages for a while. They have been with me ever since. Thank you for those roads revealed; we keep walking.
