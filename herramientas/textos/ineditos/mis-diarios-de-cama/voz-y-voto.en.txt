In this novel, with the help of literature and of Ana Paula, its protagonist, I managed to exorcise some painful demons and experiences. The most ordinary life, the reefs that the tides of existence bring, can also be a source of inspiration. Of course, it is always worth making clear that the portraits born of such impulses do not necessarily reflect those tides exactly as they strike, nor do they offer us the relief of a happy ending.
These pages were not easy to write, but writing them was imperative. I hope they are not hard to read. Given the dense and at times hostile nature of what is told, I tried to balance the necessary doses of weight with the necessary counterweights (humour, eroticism, phantasmagoria), so that they would steady the story without taking away its seriousness. As the master Eduardo Heras León taught me in his classes, one should not make the poor reader suffer. At least not gratuitously, if it can be avoided. So it is possible to tell something hard without turning it into a painful, indigestible acid. For the shadows too, for searching out those thorny places of the human condition and of real life, a novel can be the ideal vessel.
The other thing worth noting is that the text is written in the first person, in a woman's voice. By pure biological chance, the author is a man. I seem to remember that, in one of his famous lines, Groucho Marx said that men were women who hadn't made it. An interesting thought in these times of so many people unhappy with their gender, their sex and their bodies, and of so many transitions and changes of every kind.
For this writer, very happy with his male gender, with binary biology and with his heterosexuality, getting under a woman's skin turned out to be a demanding and rewarding journey. Telling Ana Paula's fears, loves, joys and sorrows was a rigorous exercise, not free of doubts and daring. Simply managing, I hope with some success, to transform myself on the page and speak and act from a woman's reasoning and feelings was reward enough. Any act that leaves worthwhile written pages behind is an achievement, at least for writers.
For now, this text will go on resting, as it must, until the time comes for its publication. Some day, its readers will have the last word.
