The novel follows a single day in the life of Juan Pérez. A forty-three-year-old journalist living in the capital of a very peculiar island in the Antilles, in the second decade of the twenty-first century, he carries a portrait of the exceptional, almost surreal daily life around him. Through realism, drama, reflection, some humour and even the absurd, the longings, setbacks and limits Juan Pérez navigates over two full turns of the clock come into view. Above all, the novel is about the loves and searches that keep its protagonist going in his daily struggle.
In a way, the book is an oblique homage to Aleksandr Solzhenitsyn's Ivan Denisovich. As a detail perhaps worth noting, and more a matter of taste than of influence, there is an explicit nod to the Guillermo Cabrera Infante of Three Trapped Tigers.
At one very specific moment of the plot, the book also pays tribute to the styles of Jorge Luis Borges, of Luis Rogelio Nogueras (an important Cuban poet and novelist) and of Cabrera Infante himself, as well as to certain recurring and never extinct formulas of a particular national journalism of that same island.
A rainy day, with memories, good and bad moments, friends, loves and dreams, is what these pages offer. A day, above all, that begins and ends full of questions.
